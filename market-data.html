Zoning, entitlement and housing supply, by the numbers

Housing starts and permits, housing-shortage estimates, new-home cost shares, entitlement value stages, and upzoning effects, from published sources.

Two different kinds of number sit behind this name, and they must not be mixed. The first kind measures the housing pipeline: starts, permits, and completions, counted by the Census Bureau each month. The second kind measures the value of permission itself: what share of a new home's price is the finished lot, what share is regulation, and how much a parcel gains as it moves from raw land to tentative approval to full entitlement. The first kind tells a reader how much is being built. The second tells a reader why the right to build is worth paying for.

What the latest numbers show is a pipeline under pressure and a permission market that still rewards certainty: September 2026 starts and permits both fell year over year, while the entitlement ladder still prices a fully entitled parcel at roughly two-thirds more than the same land raw — about 100% of potential value against about 60% — before any finished-lot premium.

How to read these numbers

Included: Freddie Mac's Q3 2024 shortage estimate; Census Bureau / HUD September 2026 starts, permits, and completions; National Association of Home Builders 2024 cost shares and 2026 regulation share; practitioner entitlement value, cost, and duration stages; the Auckland synthetic-control upzoning study; and California accessory-unit and planning-target figures from legislative analyses. Excluded: shortage estimates quoted without a method or date (published estimates range from about one million to ten million depending on method, so no single alternative figure is presented as a headline here); undated practitioner claims presented as dated figures; and any blending of gross-booking, revenue, and permit definitions, which describe different things.

Practitioner entitlement figures are market-specific frameworks, not national averages, and are labeled as such wherever they appear. Forecasts and planning targets are labeled as forecasts and targets, not actuals.
